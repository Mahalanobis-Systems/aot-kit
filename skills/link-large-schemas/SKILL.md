## What it is

When a database schema is too large to load whole, let the agent find the tables and columns each question needs by exploring it, instead of retrieving once up front. Seed it with all table names plus the top retrieved columns, give it a column index, metadata probes and trial queries, favor recall and cap the turns low; over a catalog of many databases, let it search and inspect before it chooses (exact database sets 53.52% against 21.88% for a one-shot pick, averaged over seven current models, in one study not matched for compute). First check that the schema really is too large, and compress it before deciding: in one independent comparison the whole schema gave GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Pro their best accuracy at every size tested, and writing repeated columns once and collapsing sharded tables cut 1,000- to 10,000-column schemas from 166K to 14.3K tokens and raised Claude Opus 4.7 from 12.6% to 20.7%. The limits: an independent rerun of AutoLink kept every needed column on 31.55% of the largest Spider 2.0-Snow schemas (its authors report 91.2% on Spider 2.0-Lite), exploring took 45 to 131 s per question with self-hosted 14B models, and in one company's warehouse agent a reasoning model overran its exploration budget on 77% of traces and changed its tables between repeats on 41 of 50 questions.

## When it applies

- **In the code:** a schema too large to load whole, or over about 1,000 columns with an economy or open model, linked by a single up-front retrieval or sent whole in every prompt; a raw schema full of repeated column groups or families of identically shaped sharded tables, sent or linked uncompressed; databases in a multi-database catalog picked in one call from a few retrieved candidates; a linking or exploration step in front of a schema that fits a flagship model's window, with no full-schema comparison
- **In traces:** queries against non-existent columns or the wrong tables on a large schema; prompt tokens dominated by the schema; schema prompts that overflow the window; exploration runs that end at the turn cap, overrun the schema-lookup budget or spend tens of seconds linking per question; different tables chosen for the same question across runs
- **In eval results:** strict schema-linking recall and execution accuracy on the large-schema subset, with no full-schema (raw or compressed) or one-shot retrieval baseline, or from a single run per question

## How (outline)

1. Start from the baselines: count the schema's tokens as you would send it (names, types, descriptions, sample values) against the model's window, and run the full schema (where it fits, 5.b.i) and a one-shot column retrieval on the same questions before adding exploration. In one independent comparison, the full schema gave GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Pro their best accuracy at every schema size tested (0.37 to 0.58), no linking method beat it by more than a point under 1,000 columns for any model class, and linking (done by GPT-4.1) paid only for economy and open generators on larger schemas (0.29 against 0.19 and 0.16 at 1,000 to 2,500 columns).
2. Before you decide the schema is too large, compress it once, offline: write each repeated group of columns (audit, status) once, collapse families of identically shaped sharded tables into one template, and replace long column descriptions with short shared tags. In one study on Spider 2.0-Snow with Claude Opus 4.7, this cut databases of 1,000 to 10,000 columns from 166K to 14.3K input tokens and raised execution accuracy from 12.6% to 20.7%; at 10,000 or more columns the raw 2.6M tokens fit no model tested, while the compressed 34.7K gave 63.1% strict recall and 14.5% accuracy; under 1,000 columns it changed little (33.8% to 35.1%). It also stacks with exploration: on databases over 5,000 columns it cut AutoLink's tokens from 61.0K to 26.2K and raised its accuracy from 19.6% to 21.4% (DeepSeek-V3.2).
3. Build a column-level vector index of the schema (1.4 s per database on one H100 in one study) and check what it finds on your own questions: on enterprise schemas of 244 to 971 tables with names and keys only, off-the-shelf search put every needed table in its top 15 for 21.8% to 41.0% of questions, a zero-shot 8B reranker over the top 100 for 50.3% to 77.1%, and a scorer trained on the customer's own example questions for 69.2% to 89.5%. Seed each question with all table names plus the top retrieved columns (strict recall 79.2% with the top 5 columns, 91.2% with the top 100) rather than starting from nothing: an untrained 4B model exploring with no schema in context lost 17.0 points on BIRD against having the schema (an 8B model lost 2.0).
4. If the catalog spans many databases, choose the databases first, and let the agent search and inspect catalog evidence (schemas, capabilities, relationships) over several turns rather than pick from a few retrieved ones in one call: averaged over seven current models, Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol among them, an iterative router found the exact set of needed databases for 53.52% of 815 mostly multi-database tasks, against 21.88% for a one-shot pick after inspecting up to three retrieved databases (not compute-matched, no token counts). Run over a 53-database, 102,341-column catalog with no target database given, AutoLink used 635K to 652K tokens per question, and in that study removing the database-shortlisting step lowered column recall in all six settings.
5. Give the agent actions to retrieve more columns with its own queries (removing this cost 6.7 to 7.6 points of strict recall, the largest ablation), to probe metadata and sample values with exploratory SQL (results capped at a few rows, with a timeout, 2.c.i), and to test sufficiency with a minimal executable query before it writes the answer (in one pilot, making the agent commit to a verified schema first cut hallucinated tables and columns from 26.4% of failures to 2.8%). Favor recall, then keep the set small: in a regression over 183,825 generated queries, column recall dominated accuracy (coefficient 3.382, against 0.096 for column precision), yet the linked set's size still cost accuracy (-0.429 per log column count). AutoLink's linked sets ran at 8.00% field precision in an independent rerun, held 9.5 to 24.9 times more columns than a hierarchical linker, and raised SQL-generation tokens (65.82K against 45.53K to 52.41K), so track the set's size next to recall.
6. Cap exploration at a few turns (strict recall 89.2% at 4 turns against 91.2% at 10, with 3.72 to 5.79 turns used) and budget for latency: AutoLink took 45 to 131 s of linking per question on BIRD-dev with self-hosted 14B models, against about 4 s for a single linking prompt. A reasoning model now uses the tools but tends to over-explore: AutoLink's authors avoided one because it broke the action format, while in one company's warehouse agent a reasoning configuration made 6.4 schema lookups per trace against 0.77 for a faster one with the same tools, stopped refusing (0% against 73%) and used the required table in 50% of scored finance runs against 0%, but overran its schema-exploration budget on 77% of traces, hit the tool-round limit on 16% of runs, took 119 s against 41 s on average, and changed its table set or reading of the question across three repeats on 41 of 50 questions.
7. Measure with paired runs on your own large schemas, split by schema size, against the full schema (raw and compressed, where it fits) and one-shot retrieval, with the production model and a cheaper one, repeating each question at least three times: strict linking recall (questions whose linked set holds every needed column; for a catalog, the exact set of needed databases), linked-set size, execution accuracy, whether the same tables are chosen across repeats, schema lookups and runs that end at a budget, tokens for linking and for generation, and wall-clock per question.

## How to measure

- **experiment** (Tier 3): paired runs on the same questions over your own large schemas, split by schema size (columns), with the production model and a cheaper one: the full schema raw and compressed where it fits, one-shot column retrieval, and exploration, each question run at least three times; report strict linking recall (for a multi-database catalog, the exact set of needed databases), linked-set size, execution accuracy, whether the same tables are chosen across repeats, tokens for linking and for generation, and wall-clock per question
- **monitor**: in production traces: queries that reference tables or columns outside the linked set or the schema, schema lookups per question, exploration turns and runs ending at the turn or tool-round cap, different tables chosen for the same or near-identical questions, linked-set size, the schema's share of prompt tokens, and linking wall-clock per question

## Expected impact

- **Quality:** mixed, large: exploring a catalog over several turns found the exact set of needed databases for 53.52% of 815 tasks against 21.88% for a one-shot pick, averaged over seven current models including Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol (Luo, Oct 2026); AutoLink kept every needed column on 91.2% of Spider 2.0-Lite questions against 64.0% for the best full-schema linker (Wang, Nov 2025), but an independent rerun on Spider 2.0-Snow got 73.11% overall and 31.55% on schemas of 506 or more fields, and its linked schemas gave 32.88% execution accuracy against 36.75% for a non-iterative linker (Zheng, May 2026); against sending the whole schema, linking lost for GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Pro at every size and won only for economy and open models on schemas of 1,000 or more columns (0.29 against 0.19 and 0.16 at up to 2,500) (Luoma, May 2026); compressing a 1,000- to 10,000-column schema raised Claude Opus 4.7 from 12.6% to 20.7%, and AutoLink from 19.6% to 21.4% on schemas over 5,000 columns (Liu, Jun 2026); an untrained 4B model exploring with no schema lost 17.0 points on BIRD, while an RL-trained one matched the full schema (64.9% against 64.8%) (Jian, Mar 2026); in a production warehouse agent, a reasoning configuration that explored more used the required table in 50% of scored finance runs against 0% (Kolli, Aug 2026)
- **Cost:** mixed, medium: 21.2K against 171.9K tokens per question for full-schema linkers on Spider 2.0-Lite (Wang, Nov 2025); in a rerun on Spider 2.0-Snow, 77.93K linking tokens against 240.34K and 580.80K for two other linkers, but its larger linked schemas raised generation tokens to 65.82K against 45.53K to 52.41K (Zheng, May 2026); 635K to 652K tokens per question over a 53-database catalog (Xu, Aug 2026); under 1,000 columns, LLM-based linkers used more tokens than sending the full schema (Luoma, May 2026); compressing the schema first cut AutoLink from 61.0K to 26.2K tokens per question on schemas over 5,000 columns, and a raw 1,000- to 10,000-column schema from 166K to 14.3K (Liu, Jun 2026)
- **Performance:** degrades, large: AutoLink's linking took 45 to 131 s per question on BIRD-dev with self-hosted 14B models against about 4 s for a single linking prompt, and 465 to 638 s on a 53-database Spider 2.0-Snow catalog (Xu, Aug 2026); an agentic column-by-column linker took 32 to 352 s per question and was infeasible above 2,500 columns (Luoma, May 2026); a trained 4B exploring agent took 0.6 s against 0.4 s with the schema in context (Jian, Mar 2026); in a production warehouse agent, the configuration that explored more took 119 s per question on average (p90 234 s) against 41 s (86 s) and hit the tool-round limit on 16% of runs (Kolli, Aug 2026)
- **Governance:** degrades, small: in one company's warehouse agent, the reasoning configuration that made 6.4 schema lookups per trace changed its table set or interpretation across three repeats on 41 of 50 questions (21 of 50 for the faster configuration) and used a non-canonical source-of-truth table on 15 of 30 finance runs, though both configurations avoided a prohibited table on every scored run (Kolli, Aug 2026); one company, two anonymized model configurations, not a test with exploration switched off

## Caveats

No study yet compares exploration with the full schema on current frontier thinking models at matched compute: the catalog result on Claude Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol compares iterative routing with a one-shot pick (not compute-matched, no token counts), and the Claude Opus 4.7 result compares a raw schema with a compressed one, in the compression method's own paper, not with exploration. The case for exploring within a database still rests mostly on AutoLink's own ablations (Spider 2.0-Lite, a DeepSeek-V3 policy): a competing team's rerun found it kept every needed field on 31.55% of questions over schemas of 506 or more fields, and in the one independent comparison against the full schema (eight linking methods, none an exploration agent, GPT-4.1 doing the linking) the full schema won for GPT-4.1 and Gemini 2.5 Pro at every size, on large schemas drawn mostly from 221 Spider 2 questions and padded Bird schemas. Exploration costs time and needs a capable, bounded policy: latency comes from self-hosted 14B models and from one company's warehouse agent (two anonymized configurations, 50 questions, three repeats), where the reasoning configuration over-explored and changed its tables between runs, and an untrained 4B model exploring from no schema lost 17.0 points. Security is unmeasured: no source tests what sample-value probes expose or how read-only roles and row-level permissions hold up under an agent's exploratory SQL.
